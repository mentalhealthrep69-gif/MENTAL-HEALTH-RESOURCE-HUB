## 1. What is Mental Health?

**Mental health** is your overall emotional, psychological, and social well-being. It affects how you think, feel, act, handle stress, and relate to other people.

Just like physical health, everyone has mental health—and it changes day by day.

### 3 Steps to Check Your Mental Health
1. **Check Your Mind:** Are your thoughts mostly clear, or do you feel constant brain fog and racing worry?
2. **Check Your Feelings:** Can you navigate daily ups and downs, or do you feel stuck in extreme emotions?
3. **Check Your Body & Habits:** Are you able to sleep, eat well, and stay connected with people around you?

## 2. Mental Health vs. Mental Illness

People often confuse these two terms, but they are not the same thing.

| Feature | Mental Health | Mental Illness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **What it is** | Your daily emotional state and well-being. | A diagnosed medical condition that changes how you think and feel. |
| **Who has it?** | Everyone. | Some people (about 1 in 4 people experience one). |
| **Example** | Feeling stressed before an exam, then feeling fine after. | Experiencing ongoing depression or severe panic disorder for months. |

### Steps to Understand the Difference
1. **Look at Time:** Stress and sad feelings are normal for a few days. Mental illness lasts for weeks or months without stopping.
2. **Look at Impact:** Poor mental health makes a day feel hard. Mental illness makes basic daily activities (like getting out of bed or attending class) feel nearly impossible.
3. **Look at the Solution:** Poor mental health improves with rest, self-care, and support. Mental illness often requires guidance from a professional counselor or doctor.

## 3. Common Mental Health Conditions Among Students

University life brings new pressures. Here are four common challenges students face:

### 1. Anxiety Disorders
* **What it is:** Constant, intense fear or worry that does not go away.
* **How it feels:** Racing heart, sweating, and avoiding classes, presentations, or social events due to fear.

### 2. Depression
* **What it is:** A persistent feeling of deep sadness, emptiness, and lack of energy.
* **How it feels:** Losing interest in hobbies, feeling hopeless, and struggling to get out of bed.

### 3. Academic Burnout
* **What it is:** Total physical, mental, and emotional exhaustion caused by long periods of unmanaged study stress.
* **How it feels:** Feeling completely drained, unable to concentrate, and feeling like your efforts do not matter.

### 4. Panic Attacks
* **What it is:** A sudden wave of intense physical fear that peaks within minutes.
* **How it feels:** Shortness of breath, chest tightness, dizziness, and feeling like you are losing control.

## 4. Common Signs Someone May Be Struggling

When someone is going through a hard time, their behavior will usually change.

### 4 Steps to Spot the Signs
1. **Look for Emotional Changes:** Frequent crying, sudden anger, constant fear, or seeming completely numb and emotionless.
2. **Look for Behavioral Changes:** Skipping classes, pulling away from friends, stopping hobbies, or staying in room all day.
3. **Look for Physical Changes:** Looking constantly tired, big changes in weight/appetite, or neglecting basic personal hygiene.
4. **Look for Academic Changes:** Missing assignment deadlines, sudden drop in grades, or inability to focus during lectures.

## 5. Myths vs. Facts About Mental Health

Misinformation creates stigma and stops students from getting help. Here are the facts:

| ❌ Myth | ✅ Fact |
| :--- | :--- |
| *"Mental health struggles are a sign of weakness."* | **Fact:** Mental health issues are health conditions, just like asthma or malaria. They have nothing to do with weakness. |
| *"You can just snap out of depression if you stay positive."* | **Fact:** You cannot simply will away a mental health condition. Recovery takes time, self-care, and proper support. |
| *"Therapy is only for crazy people."* | **Fact:** Therapy is a normal, healthy tool that helps anyone manage stress, process emotions, and build coping skills. |
| *"Talking about mental health makes things worse."* | **Fact:** Speaking openly breaks isolation, lowers stress, and helps people realize they are not alone. |

> 🌱 **Key Message:** Taking care of your mind is just as important as taking care of your body. Asking for help is a smart and healthy choice.
