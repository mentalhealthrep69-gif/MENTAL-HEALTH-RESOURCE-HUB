# ⚡ Stress vs. Anxiety

Stress and anxiety feel very similar in your body, but they come from different sources. 

While both activate your body's survival mechanisms (increasing heart rate and focus), understanding whether you are experiencing external stress or internal anxiety helps you choose the right tools to manage it.

---

## ⚡ The Difference
* **Stress:** Caused by a clear external problem (like an upcoming test, assignment deadline, or financial issue). Once the event passes or the problem is solved, the stress usually goes away.
* **Anxiety:** A lasting internal feeling of fear, dread, or worry about what *might* happen. It can stick around even when things are calm or when no immediate threat exists.

---

## 📊 Quick Comparison

| Feature | Stress | Anxiety |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Trigger** | External stressor (exams, work, conflict). | Internal reaction (overthinking, anticipation of danger). |
| **Duration** | Short-term; fades after the event ends. | Persistent; can continue long after the stressor is gone. |
| **Sensation** | Feeling overwhelmed, rushed, or pressured. | Feeling uneasy, fearful, or hyper-vigilant. |

---

## 🛠️ Management Strategies

### For Stress:
1. **Time Management:** Break large tasks into smaller, actionable steps.
2. **Problem Solving:** Address the immediate external trigger directly.
3. **Rest & Recovery:** Take deliberate study breaks to let your body reset.

### For Anxiety:
1. **Grounding Exercises:** Use the 5-4-3-2-1 sensory technique to stay anchored in the present moment.
2. **Deep Breathing:** Slow, rhythmic breathing calms an overactive nervous system.
3. **Challenge Thoughts:** Ask yourself if your worries are based on immediate facts or future assumptions.

---

> **🌱 Remember:** Both stress and anxiety are natural human experiences. When managed with healthy coping techniques, you can regain control and clarity.
